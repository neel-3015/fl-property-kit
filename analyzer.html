How the model works (and what it does NOT do)

  • Property tax (investment): non-homestead, no Save-Our-Homes cap — assessed value rises with the market. Optional 15% assessed-value deduction toggle reflects the 2026-era non-homestead reduction that can apply in some circumstances — verify applicability with the county property appraiser.
  • Property tax (primary residence): homestead exemption applied ($50K under current law; Amendment 3 on the Nov 2026 ballot would raise it to $150K in 2027 and $250K in 2028 if approved by 60% of voters) + Assessment Limitation ("Save Our Homes"): assessed value growth capped at min(3%, market growth). Selling decaptures the benefit — the capped assessed value resets toward market for the next buyer, and you lose the cap when you move.
  • Not modeled: income taxes / depreciation (real but outside this model), mortgage-rate changes on fixed loans, flood insurance zone specifics, condo milestone/SIRS special assessments (can add $10K–$100K+ one-time), HOA assessment spikes, hurricane deductible timing, vacancy at turnover between tenants beyond the 5% factor.
  • Effective millage defaults (all-in ≈ 1.85% Brevard / 1.90% Orange) are approximations for non-homestead property. Confirm exact millage on the parcel at pabrevard.org / ocpafl.org.
